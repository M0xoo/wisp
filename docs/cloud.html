In the preview today: your own wisp on a server.

What works right now at grid.trywisp.dev. The sections after it are where Cloud is going.

Its own sandbox

Your wisp runs in a gVisor container of its own, with its own folder and network, firewalled from the host and from everyone else.

Sign in with GitHub or Google

You get your wisp at you.grid.trywisp.dev, and an account page with your devices and how you sign in.

Your own computers, from anywhere

In wisp on your desktop, Machines → Reach this computer from anywhere. It holds a tunnel to the grid, so your phone and your other computers open it with no SSH, Tailscale or open port. The phone app lists your cloud wisp and every computer you added.

Your agents, your accounts

Every agent is already installed: Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Grok, Antigravity, opencode. Sign in from the model picker or Settings → Agents with a link and a code, GitHub too. The logins stay in your box, and the picker only offers what you signed in to.

Sleeps, then wakes

Nothing running and nobody looking: it stops. It wakes in seconds when you open it, send it a task, or a schedule is due.

Or run your own

The grid is in the wisp repo (MIT). One small Linux server runs it for a team: npx wisp-agents grid init. How →

Managed machines, without the SSH.

Coming. wisp already runs agents on your own boxes over SSH. Cloud machines show up in the same Run on menu, except we boot them, keep the harness CLIs current and take them down when the queue is empty. Every task still gets its own worktree, diffs, rollback and Ship it.

Ready in seconds

Warm images with Node, git, gh and all six harness CLIs. A machine boots, clones and starts the task before you've switched tabs.

Scale to zero

Machines sleep when the queue is empty and wake on the next task, a schedule or a CI failure. You pay for minutes that ran agents.

Your keys, your repos

Harnesses sign in with your own Claude, OpenAI or Cursor accounts. Repos come in through a GitHub App scoped to the ones you pick.

A queue that works the night shift.

Your queue lives in the cloud, not in a process on your laptop. Tasks you queue from your phone, recipes on a schedule and CI-failure triggers keep landing and running while you're away. Drag through one night.

      Questions

      Will the local CLI ever need an account?

      No. npx wisp-agents keeps working with no sign-in, no telemetry you haven't turned on, and no feature moved behind Cloud. Cloud is opt-in from Settings, like every integration.

      How many people can use the preview?

      It grows with its server: a new seat opens when the server shows it has room for one more person, so everyone's wisp stays quick. When they're all taken, leave your email: the next seat is held for the oldest email on the list. Already signed up? Your account keeps working.

      Do you resell model usage?

      No. Your cloud wisp signs in with your own Claude, ChatGPT or GitHub accounts, from Settings → Agents. The preview is free.

      How is this different from Machines?

      Machines connects wisp to computers you already run, over SSH, and stays free. A cloud computer is one we run for you: your own sandboxed wisp that sleeps when nothing's going on and wakes for you or a schedule. And with the grid, Machines needs no SSH: add a computer to it and your other devices reach it through the grid.

      Does what goes to my computer pass through the grid?

      Yes, the way your cloud wisp's does: over HTTPS to the grid, which checks it's you, then down the tunnel your computer holds open. The grid forwards it and keeps nothing of it. If you'd rather it never leaves your own network, use Machines over SSH or Tailscale instead.

      Where does my code live?

      In your own sandbox on the grid: a gVisor container with its own folder, which only you reach. Your agents' logins stay in that folder too. Delete your account and the folder and its keys are gone.

      Can I answer questions from my phone?

      Yes: open your cloud computer from the phone app, or have ask_user questions sent to ntfy or Telegram. Your answer goes straight back to the agent.

      Does the phone app need an account?

      Sign in to wisp Cloud once, with a code, on its first screen: your cloud wisp and every computer you added to the grid are listed there. Without an account, it still connects to your computers over SSH (folded under Connect over SSH instead).

      Can I host it myself?

      Yes. The grid is in the wisp repo (MIT): npx wisp-agents grid init on a small Linux server, a GitHub or Google OAuth app, and two DNS records. Step by step.

      What happens when I sign a device out?

      Remove it on your account page at grid.trywisp.dev: its token stops working at once, and a computer it added leaves the grid. Your other devices and running tasks keep going.

      Can I mix local and cloud?

      Yes. Run on lists this computer, your cloud wisp, your computers on the grid and your SSH machines together, each marked by kind. Pick one, or several at once.
